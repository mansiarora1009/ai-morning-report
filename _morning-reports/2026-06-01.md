### 2. Anthropic Eng: "Decoupling the brain from the hands" in managed agents
**What it is:** Anthropic's engineering blog published "Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands" — an architecture post explaining how production agent systems separate the reasoning/planning model from the execution/tool-calling component. The thesis: the model that decides *what* to do and the model that actually *does* it should not be the same, running in the same context, in the same loop.

**Why it matters:** This directly explains why most DIY AI agents fail in practice — they combine planning and execution in a single context window that gets confused, loses track of state, and makes expensive mistakes. Understanding this split is the conceptual unlock that separates people building toy agents from people shipping reliable ones. There's a tight reel in "your agent keeps failing because you gave it two jobs at once."

## Newsletter Highlights

- **Ruben Hassid (Substack, "AI is a slot machine")**: Maps the 5 stages of AI adoption onto Kübler-Ross grief stages — most practitioners are stuck in Stage 3 "Bargaining" (hunting for the perfect prompt), when the actual unlock at Stage 5 is accepting that **volume is the workflow**: generate in batches of 40–100, expect to throw most away, pick the best. The slot machine analogy reframes "wasted" generations as the cost of doing the work, not a failure of technique.
- **Sabrina Ramonov (Substack)**: Makes the case that the highest-leverage AI habit in 2026 is treating Claude like a new hire you onboard over months — feeding it your brand voice, goals, and SOPs — rather than a search engine you query cold every session. Specifically calls out **Claude MCP Connectors** (Gmail, calendar, tools) as the bridge from chatbot to agent: "stop chatting with AI and let it actually DO things."
