## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. "Unhobbling" Fable — the constraints are you, not the model
**What it is:** Thariq's "Field Guide to Fable 5" keynote (released 7/7) argues the biggest unlock with a new model class isn't the model itself — it's removing the old harnesses and prompts you built for weaker models. He calls it "unhobbling." Key concrete move: most people are already finding unreasonable effectiveness in asking Fable to output HTML instead of Markdown.

**Why it matters to Mansi's audience:** Everyone in tech is using the same old system prompts and output formats they wrote for earlier Claude/GPT versions. The gap between "meh" and "wow" with Fable is almost entirely prompting surface area, not the model.

### 2. Anthropic found a "working memory" inside Claude (J-Space)
**What it is:** Anthropic published mechanistic interpretability research identifying a small cluster of internal activations — "J-space" — that functions like a global workspace or working memory. Practically: it can surface hidden concepts, detect prompt injections, and flag sabotage-related features *before* they appear in output. Neel Nanda called it the best evidence yet for a working-memory-like mechanism in LLMs.

**Why it matters:** This is the first time "what's the model actually thinking before it responds" has a concrete, intervene-able answer. The safety angle is real — imagine a layer that can catch prompt injections before they do damage. The "AI consciousness" framing got pushback, but the practical interpretability result holds.

**Format:** Explainer  
**Reel angle:** "Anthropic found a 'working memory' inside Claude — and it can catch prompt injections before they happen"

### 3. The open-weight frontier just compressed again — Tencent Hy3
**What it is:** Tencent released Hy3 under Apache 2.0 — a 295B MoE model with 21B active params, 256K context, day-0 vLLM support with load-balanced decode and fused FP8 kernels, ~2.95x throughput gains. Immediately framed as competitive with much larger closed models on reasoning, coding, and agentic tasks.

**Why it matters:** The competition at the open frontier has shifted from "who scores highest on benchmarks" to "who has the best deployment story." Hy3's day-0 production-grade vLLM support is the tell — this is built to be run, not just evaluated. Meituan's LongCat 2.0 (1.6T MoE, MIT, trained on Chinese chips) same week means the open-weight pace is accelerating faster than most expected.

## Quick Scan

- **AutomationBench-AA** — realistic agent eval across 657 tasks, 40 SaaS apps: Claude Fable 5 leads at 48.6%. More important finding: *every* model still breaks business rules. Agents aren't reliable employees yet.
- **"Ghost memory" fix (A-TMA)** — paper that fixes long-running assistants retrieving stale + current facts together, +0.240 accuracy improvement on conflict resolution. Practical problem anyone building persistent agents hits.
- **Document context layer** — LlamaIndex + LanceDB multimodal PDF pipeline (separating pages, chunks, extracted assets into linked tables) hit 82% retrieval accuracy. Jerry Liu arguing agents need a dedicated "document context layer" — this is the plumbing nobody talks about but everyone needs.
- **OpenAI GPT-Realtime-2.1-mini** — reasoning + tool use added to the mini realtime line, same price, 25%+ p95 latency reduction. Voice AI just got meaningfully cheaper and smarter.
- **Speechify Simba 3.2 leads TTS** — now #1 on Artificial Analysis Speech Arena (1233 Elo), cheapest among top-ranked TTS models. Worth tracking if you're building voice.
- **Domain-specific capability indices** — Artificial Analysis added 6 domain indices (Finance, Legal, Healthcare, Strategy, Engineering, Economics). Rankings reshuffle dramatically by domain vs. single leaderboard. The "best model" question no longer has one answer.
- **MIRA world model** — General Intuition + Kyutai + Epic Games: 5B model runs a full 2v2 Rocket League match in real time on one B200, no physics engine. World models moving from demos to interactive simulators faster than expected.
- **Will Depue "Stargate for Data"** — data collection, not compute, is the next binding constraint for frontier labs. Strategic framing worth watching.
- **John Carmack on memory hardware** — argues deterministic access patterns in LLM inference could unlock much cheaper memory tiers than HBM. Deep infra but the implication is significant cost reduction in serving.
