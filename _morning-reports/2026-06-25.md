## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Claude Tag — AI stops being a tool, starts being a coworker
**What it is:** Anthropic launched Claude Tag, a Slack-native agent where Claude gets its own identity, credentials, and auditable action log — treated as an actual org member you `@tag`, not a chatbot you visit.

**Why it matters:** @karpathy called this an "org-level harness," not a Slack bot. @gallabytes described the experiential jump from Claude Code (pairing partner) to Claude Tag (managing a team). The tension: Claude now has its own login, bills by the thought, and remembers everything — raising real concerns about tacit-knowledge lock-in, prompt-injection risk, and budget opacity. Hugging Face responded by publishing their own open-source self-hosted equivalent (Moon Bot) to avoid vendor capture.

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "The moment AI stopped being a tool you visit and became a coworker with a login."

### 2. OpenAI's Jalapeño Chip — the real AI war is about who owns the compute stack
**What it is:** OpenAI announced Jalapeño, its first custom AI inference chip built with Broadcom, reportedly designed in just 9 months (partly with help from OpenAI's own models). Estimated specs: ~216GB HBM3E, ~7.1–7.4 TB/s bandwidth, ~10 PFLOPS FP4.

**Why it matters:** This isn't just a hardware story — it's a vertical integration story. Every frontier lab wants to own chips → kernels → memory → networking → deployment, so compute economics and product behavior stop being hostage to NVIDIA supply. Same day: Qualcomm acquired Modular (Chris Lattner's compiler company), signaling real competition to CUDA beyond academic exercises.

**Format:** Explainer
**Reel angle:** "Why OpenAI building its own chip actually changes AI pricing for everyone."

---

### 3. Meta-Harnesses — every AI-native team is independently building the same thing
**What it is:** A new pattern the AI community calls "meta-harnesses" — orchestration layers that sit above individual AI tools (Claude Code, Cursor, etc.) to standardize, secure, and scale agent workflows across a company. Databricks CTO Matei Zaharia is betting on Omnigent, an open-source pluggable architecture for this. Cloudflare, Vercel, and others are building competing versions.

**Why it matters:** swyx's framing: "it is currently being independently rediscovered at 1000 AI-native shops." This is what MCP did for tools — meta-harnesses are doing for multi-agent orchestration. The question is whether an open standard emerges or a vendor captures it.

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "Every company building AI workflows is secretly reinventing the same missing layer — here's what it is."
