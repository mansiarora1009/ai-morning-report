### 2. The orchestrator is the new prompt — loops beat one-shots
**What it is:** A theme crystallizing across multiple sources this week: Cursor's blog ("40% of our internal PRs come from cloud agents" + lessons from building cloud agents), Anthropic Engineering's new post on Scaling Managed Agents ("decoupling the brain from the hands"), and AI practitioners on social media summing it up as "I don't prompt Claude anymore, I write loops." The unit of work is shifting from a single prompt to a control loop with branching, verification, and retry logic.

**Why it matters:** This is the gap between people who get 2x from AI and people who get 10x. One-shot prompting hits a ceiling. Harnesses — even basic ones with loops and condition checks — unlock a different tier of results.

**Format:** Explainer — "what's a harness and why do the people getting 10x all use one?"

**Reel angle:** "The people getting 10x from AI aren't better at prompting. They stopped prompting."

### 3. The AI "budget crisis" is a broken yardstick problem
**What it is:** *This Week in AI* made a sharp argument this week: the current panic about companies hitting AI spending limits is a measurement problem, not a structural one. Token usage was adopted as a proxy for AI value (like page hits for early web, or app downloads for mobile), and now companies are hitting the ceiling of that misaligned incentive. The newsletter argues: inference costs will fall, monthly flat pricing will return, and the companies treating this as "AI is over" are measuring the wrong thing.

**Why it matters:** Your audience is likely hearing the "AI budget crisis" discourse. This is the counter-frame worth having — and it comes with a practical implication: the "AI Auditor" role (someone who manages model/API/compute allocation against business outcomes, not token spend) is about to become a real job.

**Format:** Explainer — a clear "why everyone's reading this wrong" take.

**Reel angle:** "Companies 'running out of AI budget' isn't a crisis. Here's what's actually happening."
