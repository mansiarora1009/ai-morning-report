## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. The "Unhobbling" Shift — Your Old Prompts May Now Be the Bottleneck
**What it is:** The keynote argument around Fable 5's launch is that the constraints builders added to earlier models — over-specified instructions, safety guardrails, rigid output schemas — are now actively limiting what the model can do. The model leveled up; the harness didn't.

**Why it matters:** Anyone who built workflows, system prompts, or agents on earlier Claude/GPT versions and just swapped in Fable 5 is probably underperforming. The upgrade isn't "use new model in old setup." It's "redesign the setup for what the model can now actually do."

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "The thing holding your AI back right now might be the prompt you wrote 6 months ago."

---

### 2. Harness Engineering Is the New Frontier — Not Fine-Tuning
**What it is:** Lilian Weng (former OpenAI safety lead, now cofounder of Thinky) published a synthesis of 35 papers arguing that *harness optimization* — how you wrap, context, and orchestrate AI calls — is the real path to compounding capability gains. Not weight updates, not fine-tuning. Quote: "The need to specify goals and context will not disappear." LangChain launched a free open-source Deep Agents course the same day.

**Why it matters:** This is a framing shift for where to invest effort. The leverage isn't in the model anymore — it's in the system around the model. For anyone building AI workflows: what you call, in what order, with what context, is becoming the core skill.

**Format:** Explainer

**Reel angle:** "Everyone's fighting over the best model. The real edge is in the harness around it."

---

### 3. Rubric Pass Rate ≠ Task Success — A Reality Check for AI Agents
**What it is:** Artificial Analysis launched Harvey LAB-AA, a 120-task legal benchmark across 24 practice areas. Claude Fable 5 *led* — at only 14.2% all-pass rate. Models can satisfy individual checklist items while still delivering an unusable end-to-end output.

**Why it matters:** Anyone deploying AI agents for real work (legal, finance, ops, code review) needs to internalize this gap. Benchmark scores and "passes X% of criteria" tell you almost nothing about whether the output is actually useful. Evaluation design — not model choice — is the hard problem.

**Format:** Explainer

**Reel angle:** "Your AI agent can check every box and still fail the actual task. Here's why that matters."
