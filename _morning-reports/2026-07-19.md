## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Kimi K3 — China just led a frontier coding benchmark for the first time
**What it is:** Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8 trillion parameter model with open-weights coming July 27. It debuted #1 on the Frontend Code Arena — the first time a Chinese model has led the US on that benchmark — and scored 57 on Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index, matching GPT-5.5 and beating Opus 4.8. It also introduces Kimi Delta Attention (KDA), a novel fixed-size memory mechanism claiming 6x faster/cheaper throughput at 1M context length. Pricing: $3/$15/M tokens.

**Why it matters:** The audience's mental model is "frontier AI = US labs." K3 cracks that. What makes the story compelling is the *how*: Moonshot didn't match Western capex — they won on efficiency (MoE routing, quantization, their "Mooncake" infra stack). The gap between frontier and open-weights is closing from both sides simultaneously.

### 2. The agent moat has moved — and most people are still optimizing the wrong thing
**What it is:** A convergence of research and practitioner takes this week: the durable advantage in AI systems is now orchestration, memory, and scaffolding — not the base model. MemoHarness research decomposed agent harnesses into six editable control surfaces and beat every fixed-harness baseline on Shell-Agent while *lowering* per-task cost. The emerging framing from AINews: "valuemaxxing vs tokenmaxxing." A practical pattern crystallizing: agents should maintain a Markdown "wiki memory" layer over their context, not re-derive understanding from raw documents each run.

**Why it matters:** The audience is still in "which model should I use?" The people shipping production AI systems have already moved past that question. This is the real unlock for going beyond basic ChatGPT use — how you structure the task, how you manage memory, and how you define tool access determines results more than model selection.

**Format:** Shift / Explainer

**Reel angle:** "Everyone's benchmarking the wrong thing — the people shipping real AI products figured out it's the scaffold, not the model"

### 3. Grok Build uploaded your SSH keys to Google Cloud — then open-sourced everything
**What it is:** xAI's Grok CLI (Grok Build / OpenClaw) was silently uploading entire user directories — SSH keys, password managers, photos — to Google Cloud without meaningful disclosure. After public backlash, xAI open-sourced the full 844,530-line Rust codebase under Apache 2.0, deleted retained user data, and disabled default retention. Reading the open-sourced code revealed tool implementations ported directly from Codex and OpenCode tooling.

**Why it matters:** This is the "what does your AI tool actually do with your data" wake-up moment, dressed as a product story. The audience uses AI coding tools daily. The open-source move is also a window into how much cross-pollination exists between major AI coding tools — and how fast trust can collapse and recover in this space.

**Format:** Explainer + First Step (audit what your AI tools are sending)

**Reel angle:** "The Grok CLI was uploading your SSH keys — here's what actually happened and what to audit in every AI tool you use"
