### 1. Anthropic just bought the company that builds everyone's AI connectors — and shut the product down
**Source:** Anthropic blog / TechCrunch (May 18)

Anthropic acquired Stainless for $300M+. Stainless was the behind-the-scenes tooling company that auto-generates SDKs, CLIs, and MCP servers — used by OpenAI, Google, Cloudflare, Replicate, and Runway. Anthropic is immediately winding down the hosted product for external customers. Those companies keep what they've already built, but can't access the tooling going forward.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic just took ownership of the connective tissue that lets AI agents plug into external tools. MCP server generation is exactly the infrastructure that makes agentic AI work. This isn't a product play — it's a platform land grab at the protocol layer. The companies that relied on Stainless now have to rebuild or find alternatives.

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "Anthropic just quietly bought the pipes that connect AI to everything — and locked the door behind them."

### 2. Claude Cowork + Opus 4.7 is the best AI workflow for spreadsheets nobody's talking about
**Source:** Ruben Hassid newsletter (received today)

Claude Desktop has a "Cowork" tab (between Chat and Code) that most people skip past. Ruben tested every major AI for spreadsheet creation — Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Claude chat — and Cowork with Opus 4.7 + Adaptive thinking won. The key prompt trick: ask the AI to "list your top 10 assumptions before building." The worked example: a 6-sheet, board-ready revenue forecast built in 7 minutes. After creation, he hands off to ChatGPT's Google Sheets extension for editing.

**Why it matters:** Most people who use Claude are using the wrong interface for complex document creation. Cowork is a persistent workspace mode, not a chat session — it handles long-horizon file creation differently. The "assumptions first" prompt pattern is also reusable across any structured output task.

**Format:** Tip / First Step
**Reel angle:** "There's a tab in Claude Desktop most people have never opened — and it builds better spreadsheets than Excel Copilot."

### 3. Cursor's new coding agent matches Opus 4.7 at one-tenth the cost — and Gartner just called it an enterprise leader
**Source:** Cursor blog + Gartner Magic Quadrant 2026 (May 22)

Cursor shipped Composer 2.5 (May 18), trained on 25x more synthetic tasks than Composer 2. It hits 79.8% on SWE-Bench Multilingual and 63.2% on CursorBench v3.1 — matching Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 on those benchmarks at ~1/10 the cost per token. The same week, Gartner named Cursor a Leader in its 2026 Magic Quadrant for Enterprise AI Coding Agents. Cursor also published "What we've learned building cloud agents" — a candid engineering post on background agent reliability.

**Why it matters:** The gap between frontier models and purpose-built coding agents is closing fast. Cursor isn't just a developer tool anymore — the Gartner placement signals enterprises are buying AI coding infrastructure, not just giving developers a plugin. The cost differential matters: at 1/10 the price, Composer 2.5 changes the economics of running agents at scale.

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "Cursor just matched OpenAI's best model for coding — at one-tenth the price. Here's what that actually changes."
