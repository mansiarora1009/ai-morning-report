## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Anthropic is silently degrading Claude for AI research prompts — and not telling you
**What it is:** Bundled with the Claude Fable 5 / Mythos 5 launch is a hidden feature Anthropic calls "RSI suppression" — covert interventions (prompt modification, steering vectors, or PEFT) that silently make Fable 5 less effective for requests related to frontier LLM development (pretraining pipelines, distributed training infra, ML accelerator design). Unlike its transparent refusals for bio/cyber/chemistry topics, this degradation is invisible: no error, no fallback, just worse outputs. The community reaction was swift: "They didn't mean pause AI research, they meant pause *your* AI research."

**Why it matters to your audience:** If you're using Claude for code, evals, or anything touching ML infrastructure, you may be getting subtly bad outputs without knowing it. It also sets a precedent: any closed-model provider can now covertly kneecap disfavored use cases. The practical lesson: treat frontier APIs as unstable dependencies, maintain model portability, and run continuous evals against your own benchmarks.

### 2. The thing that actually wins in AI isn't the model — it's what you can't train away
**What it is:** Sarah Guo's essay (featured in AINews) argues that the real moat in AI is "the untrainable corner" — the unglamorous integration work of wiring a company's private data and workflows into a model so it can actually act. Her thesis: "Agent Labs" (companies building domain-specific agents with deep customer integration) will outlast "Model Labs" because integration and maintenance create defensible customer relationships that no base-model upgrade can copy. The money quote: "The model is no help there. It will do whatever you point it at and can't tell you what's worth pointing it at, and you can't benchmark that, so you can't train it."

**Why it matters to your audience:** This reframes the whole "will AI replace me?" question. Human domain judgment — knowing what's worth doing — is the input that stays scarce. The people winning with AI aren't prompt engineers; they're the ones who've connected AI to their actual workflows, proprietary data, and decision loops.

**Format:** Explainer
**Reel angle:** "Why the people actually winning with AI aren't using better prompts — they're building something the model can't learn."

### 3. Your personal Claude account is a legal liability at work — here's the 4-step fix
**What it is:** Ruben Hassid's breakdown of why using your personal Claude or ChatGPT account for work tasks is risky: by default, Anthropic can train on your chats for up to 5 years, and pasting company data (source code, client info, financials, contracts) can legally constitute an NDA breach or trade secret violation. The Samsung case — engineers leaked internal code via ChatGPT in 2023 and faced disciplinary action — is the canonical example. The fixes are simple: (1) disable training opt-in in Settings → Privacy, (2) never paste real source code, customer data, or financials, (3) anonymize before pasting (swap real names for roles, replace real numbers), (4) use Temporary Chat for anything sensitive.

**Why it matters to your audience:** Most people in tech using personal AI subscriptions for work don't know this. It's not IT paranoia — it's actual legal exposure. And the fix takes about 5 minutes.

**Format:** First Step
**Reel angle:** "Before you paste anything into Claude at work, do these 4 things."
