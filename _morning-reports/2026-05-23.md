### 2. Why AI Agents Are Not SaaS
**Format:** Explainer
**Why it matters:** "This Week in AI Club" laid out the most useful framework for understanding where AI agent value actually accrues — and it's counterintuitive. Agents aren't replacing software; they're replacing *coordination friction* — the hidden overhead of routing approvals, syncing systems, managing state. That changes where the moat is.

The key taxonomy (5 layers):
- **L1 — Personal Agents** (inbox, meeting notes): Easy to copy. Become features, not companies.
- **L2 — Internal Workflow Agents**: Where most AI agencies make money now. Too custom to scale as software.
- **L3 — Functional Workflow Agents**: First agents that include *judgment* — where work goes, who intervenes.
- **L4 — Cross-Functional Agents**: Company reorganizes itself around AI-native operations.
- **L5 — Organizational Agentic Platforms**: Where durable businesses form. Moat = operational lock-in, not the model. Examples: Clay, Vanta.

The bottom line: *"The agent itself is not the moat. The surrounding operational dependency is."*

**Reel angle:** "You're probably building an L1 agent. Here's why L5 is where actual businesses form."
