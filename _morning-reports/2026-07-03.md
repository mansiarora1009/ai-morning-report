## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Skill Engineering: Giving AI Agents a Design Vocabulary
**What it is:** Paul Bakaus (creator of *Impeccable*, an open-source design skills system for coding agents) introduced the concept of "skill engineering" at AIEWF. Instead of asking an agent to redesign a site "in one shot," users give it commands like "bolder," "quieter," or "denser" — each precisely defined to mean hierarchy, scale, and typography choices, not gradients and neon glow.

**Why it matters:** Your agent's vague understanding of design terms is a workflow problem, not a model problem. Skill engineering is the discipline of giving agents domain knowledge and precise vocabulary so they converge on what you actually want — not what a generic LLM thinks "bold" means.

**Reel angle:** "Why your AI keeps getting 'make it pop' wrong — and the open-source fix that's changing that"

### 2. The Loops Debate — and What Anthropic Is Actually Doing With Agents
**What it is:** The final day of AI Engineer World's Fair featured a debate: are autonomous AI "loops" (agents running code cycles end-to-end) viable now? Skeptics (Dex Horthy, HumanLayer) said "hype is outrunning discipline" and we need to step *down* an abstraction level, not up. Meanwhile, Anthropic's Mike Krieger revealed how they internally use *Claude Tag* — not as a replacement for their team, but as a multiplayer, async, proactive system where engineers delegate ownership: "Don't just fix this bug. You are now responsible for this part of the codebase."

**Why it matters:** The real shift isn't "AI writes all the code" — it's AI holding *ownership* of a domain while humans steer and review. Krieger said their bottleneck is now human review capacity, not AI output capacity. That reframe is worth understanding.

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "The AI code loop debate everyone got wrong — here's what Anthropic is actually doing internally"

### 3. Websites That Assemble Themselves Per Visitor — It's Not the Future
**What it is:** Adobe Principal Scientist Carlos Sanchez demonstrated "agentic sites" at AIEWF — web pages assembled in real time around each visitor's intent using LLMs and the site's existing content corpus. Intent signals (browsing behavior, queries) route to a page composed specifically for that visitor. Current cost: 1–2 cents per page.

**Why it matters:** "Personalization" has meant segment-based selection for 20 years. This is full runtime page assembly. Sanchez was explicit: "Many people don't even think it's possible to generate a web page on the fly. People think it is future-looking. No, you can do this. It's not the future, it's the present now."

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "A website that builds a different page for every single visitor — live, right now, for 2 cents"
