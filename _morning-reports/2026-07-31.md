### 2. Ontologies Are Back: How AI Engineers Are Keeping Agents Honest
**What it is:** At AI Engineer World's Fair, a wave of talks argued that production AI agents need "logical guardrails" — and the answer turns out to be ontologies, knowledge graphs, and structured schemas from the 2000s Semantic Web era. The pitch: LLMs are probabilistic; ontologies are deterministic; together they give you agents that can reason freely but can't violate your business rules.

**Why it matters to your audience:** Anyone building multi-step agents has hit the problem of the agent drifting or making up facts about your domain. The practical recipe emerging: (1) define your domain entities as a graph (Schema.org, OWL, or your own), (2) let the LLM reason over it, (3) use a reasoner to check the LLM's conclusions against the ontology's axioms. Established web ontologies are especially useful because LLMs already know them from training data.

**Format:** Explainer — makes "ontology" finally make sense in a practical agentic context.

**Reel angle:** *"If your AI agent keeps going off the rails, engineers are solving it with a 20-year-old web idea"*
