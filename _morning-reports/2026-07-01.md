### 2. "Loops" is the new paradigm — and it's replacing how we think about apps
**What it is:** At AI Engineer World's Fair (Day 2), every major talk converged on one word: loops. swyx opened with "Loopcraft: The Art of Stacking Loops." OpenAI, Microsoft, Factory, Warp — all framing their products as loop-builders. The core idea: AI engineering has moved from chat → tools → goals → **automations / cron jobs / loops** that run persistently and react to signals.

**Why it matters:** This isn't hype vocabulary — it's a structural shift in how AI-native software is designed. A "software factory" (Tereza Tížková, Factory) = the whole lifecycle of development with autonomy: collect signals, react to feedback, prioritize, orchestrate. Not an app. A loop.

**Format fit:** Explainer
**Reel angle:** "Everyone at AIEWF kept saying 'loops' — here's what they actually mean and why it changes how you build."

### 3. A new job role is crystallizing: the Agent Engineer
**What it is:** At AIEWF's Forward Deployed Engineering track, Sierra's Natalie Meurer made the case that FDE is converging with product engineering — and that the real new role is "agent engineer": systems integration + agent development + customer ops + product sense. Sierra deliberately chose "agent engineer" over "forward deployed engineer" because the title should reflect the shape of the technical work.

**Why it matters:** This is the earliest signal of what AI-native org charts actually look like. Not "AI researcher" or "prompt engineer" — but engineers who sit at the intersection of deployment, customer problems, and agentic systems. This is the job your audience should be angling toward (or building toward).

**Format fit:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "The AI job nobody's talking about yet — and why it's the most important one to understand."
