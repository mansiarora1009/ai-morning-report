### 2. Cost per task is replacing cost per token — and expensive models are winning because of it
**What it is:** A cluster of converging data points from the AINews recap: (1) Devin Fusion switched to Fable 5 as its lead model and found it *cheaper* than Opus 4.8 in practice — because in 81% of Fable-led runs, the lead model never makes a code edit, meaning stronger delegation avoids wasted token spend. (2) Artificial Analysis now emphasizes cost per task over token pricing because turns, verbosity, and cache rates dominate real cost. (3) skirano's coding-agent index found Terra Max competitive with Fable 5 Max on score for materially lower cost. The metric that matters is shifting.

**Why it matters:** Almost everyone in your audience is still pricing AI tools by $/million tokens. The practitioners who are actually running agents at scale have moved past that — they've realized a smarter model that does less often costs less. This is one of those "the map is wrong" moments that's very reelable.

**Format:** Explainer / Shift

**Reel angle:** "Counterintuitive: the most expensive AI model can be the cheapest to run. Here's the math that breaks your intuition"

### 3. "The harness is the app" is hardening from a vibe into a consensus
**What it is:** Multiple independent threads in the July 11–13 AI discourse converged on the same idea: the orchestrator/harness surrounding the model now determines outcomes more than the model itself. threepointone's talk was summarized as "the harness is the app." LangChain argued task-specialized harnesses beat generic wrappers. On the infra side, Prime Intellect shipped verifiers v1 — a major redesign of its RL environment stack for agentic training with message DAGs that drop trace storage from O(n²) to O(n), making long-horizon agent training practical.

**Why it matters:** Three months ago this was an insightful take. Now it's becoming received wisdom among the people shipping things. Your audience is still asking "which model should I use?" — the shift worth naming is that the answer increasingly depends on what's wrapping it.
