## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Skills are the new unit of work for AI agents — and it's already mainstream
**What it is:** At AI Engineer World's Fair 2026, "Skills" — markdown files that declare how agents should run a specific workflow — emerged as the universal abstraction across every major platform: Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Gemini CLI. Garry Tan put it plainly: "AI-native companies encode all of that as skills, written procedures that their agents execute, and they hire engineers whose job it is to maintain those skills." Philipp Schmid (Google DeepMind): "Agents are just files. We write markdown files to extend capabilities."

**Why it matters to your audience:** This reframes what AI-native work actually looks like. It's not about the model; it's about who can write better skills. Your audience is exactly the people who should be building their own.

**Format:** Shift
**Reel angle:** "The people winning with AI agents aren't writing more code — they're writing better markdown. Here's what a skill file actually looks like."

---

### 2. Harness quality is now the moat — not the model
**What it is:** swyx's AINews (July 14) flagged the emerging consensus: stale `agents.md` instructions act like self-inflicted prompt injection, causing multi-hour stalls in long-running tasks. Andy Konwinski (Databricks co-founder): "Companies that encode value into evals and environments gain a more durable edge than those relying on capital or scale alone." LangSmith now traces Codex, Cursor, and Copilot — observability for coding agents is becoming its own product category.

**Why it matters:** Your audience is starting to use coding agents seriously. They need to know that the configuration layer (context files, evals, tool permissions) matters more than which model they're running.

**Format:** Explainer
**Reel angle:** "Your AI agent isn't slow because the model is bad. It's slow because your instructions file is rotting. Here's the fix."

---

### 3. Inner loop / outer loop: the mental model everyone at AIEWF 2026 was using
**What it is:** The conference buzzword was "loops." The dominant mental model: agents own the *inner loop* (execution, iteration, tool calls), while you own the *outer loop* (direction, oversight, course-correction). Geoffrey Huntley's analogy: "We're like locomotive engineers now — our job is to keep the locomotive on the rails." Addy Osmani: "Agents can run much more of the inner execution loop, but that outer loop is still engineering."

**Why it matters:** This is one of those clarifying frames that makes a lot of confused conversations click. It explains why full autonomy isn't actually the goal — and what the human's job actually is now.

**Format:** Explainer
**Reel angle:** "Everyone's asking 'how much can I automate?' — the real question is: which loop are you in? Inner loop vs outer loop, explained."
