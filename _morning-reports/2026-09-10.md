## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Meta Muse: the first personal agent with a real security architecture
**What it is:** Meta launched Muse, a consumer personal AI agent that runs in isolated Linux VMs, mediated by a "Sentinel" layer that never exposes secrets directly to the agent and requires approval for sensitive actions. Connectors to 10+ services (Gmail, Calendar, WhatsApp, Plaid, Stripe, Instagram). Day-one usage hit 10× internal projections.

**Why it matters to your audience:** Every other agent product has swept the "but what about the security" question under the rug. Muse is the first consumer-facing product where the *architecture* is the pitch: VM isolation, a separate Sentinel process, a $300k bug bounty, and an agentic payment protection guarantee. This is the blueprint others will copy. More practically: it shifts the central question from "what can the model do?" to "what access have you granted it, and who approves what?"

**Reel angle:** *"The era of 'just trust the AI' is over — here's the security stack every personal agent will need"*

### 2. OpenAI solved a Millennium Prize math problem using 10,000 agents in 88 hours
**What it is:** An internal OpenAI model (described as "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra") proposed a solution to Navier-Stokes in 88 hours using ~10,000 parallel agents generating an estimated 130 billion output tokens. Estimated cost: $10M–$40M at API rates. Lean formalization/verification took an additional 17 hours.

**Why it matters to your audience:** The headline is wild but the meta-point is practical: *test-time compute* (running more agents, longer, in parallel) is now a first-class scaling axis — not just a curiosity. Observers note ARC-AGI costs dropped from ~$100k to ~$10 in two years; the same trajectory is beginning here. The insight for builders: your problem might not need a smarter model — it might need more parallel attempts and better orchestration.

**Format:** Shift/Explainer

**Reel angle:** *"10,000 AIs working for 88 hours just solved a 150-year-old math problem — here's what that means for your workflows"*

### 3. Harvey proved that agent harness design + fine-tuning matters more than the model
**What it is:** Harvey (AI legal) and Baseten published results from their M&A due diligence agent. Moving from a standard tool loop to a hierarchical "recursive language model" (RLM) harness — root agent delegates to sub-agents, aggregates over 80M tokens — lifted benchmark pass rate from 23% to 62%. Adding GRPO fine-tuning on Qwen3.5-122B pushed it to 63% *and* improved document coverage from 62% to 96%.

**Why it matters to your audience:** Most people are still prompting a single model. Harvey shows that the harness (how you structure agents, how sub-agents pass context, how you post-train on your own task) matters at least as much as which frontier model you pick. This is replicable at smaller scale.

**Format:** Tip/Explainer

**Reel angle:** *"Harvey went from 23% to 62% accuracy without switching models — here's what they actually changed"*
