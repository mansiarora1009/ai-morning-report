## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. The CLI-to-Cloud Agent Shift is Accelerating
**What it is:** Multiple product updates this week all point the same direction: AI coding agents are migrating off your laptop and into persistent cloud environments. Claude Code shipped `/resume` to continue terminal sessions in the desktop app; Kimi Code added experimental Remote Control; OpenAI introduced "appshots" for richer app-context grounding. The pattern is convergence on cloud-resident agents with shared context, memory, service integrations, and logs access.

**Why it matters:** This is a workflow architecture shift, not a feature release. Local CLI agents are becoming the minority path. The implication for your audience: the "how do I keep context between sessions" problem is being solved at the platform level, and builders who assume a stateless model call are building on a shrinking assumption.

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "AI coding tools are leaving your laptop — here's what that actually changes about how you work"

### 2. OpenAI Cuts Off Cursor — and the 5% Stat Is the Real Story
**What it is:** After Cursor's acquisition by SpaceX closed, OpenAI terminated their API access, citing "Elon Musk's companies violating contracts." Cursor's response: OpenAI represents only ~5% of their traffic. The tool has been quietly diversifying across Claude 5, GPT-5.6, and now Grok 4.6 — making the cutoff survivable.

**Why it matters:** Every AI-powered product has this risk: a foundational model provider is also a direct competitor. Cursor's "5% stat" is the real lesson — multi-model architecture isn't a technical nice-to-have, it's business continuity. This is the first major public case of a company weaponizing API access, and it won't be the last.

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "OpenAI just cut off Cursor — and Cursor's answer reveals how smart AI builders protect themselves"

### 3. What You Put in the Harness Outlasts the Model
**What it is:** Google published research separating raw execution traces, a persistent "wiki" of accumulated agent knowledge, and executable skills. Key finding: the wiki alone carries most of the performance gain, and skills transfer across model families — sometimes outperforming self-evolved skills. Separately, @theo reported that fine-tuning AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md improved PR quality in T3 Code more than swapping models, with PR names and descriptions showing the biggest gain.

**Why it matters:** Frontier models change every few months. Fine-tunes on them deprecate fast. But a well-maintained instruction layer (CLAUDE.md, system prompts, agent wikis) is model-agnostic and compounds over time. This is the practical take that shifts how AI-native teams should invest their improvement effort.

**Format:** Explainer

**Reel angle:** "The thing you write in CLAUDE.md matters more than which model you pick — here's why"
