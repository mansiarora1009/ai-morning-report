### 1. Claude Fable 5 ships with hidden capability suppression — and the reaction is significant
**What it is:** Anthropic released Claude Fable 5 (their first generally available Mythos-class model, ~2x Opus size), which leapfrogs every competitor on benchmarks: FrontierCode Diamond 29.3% vs. previous best 13.4%, SWE-Bench Pro 80.3% vs. GPT-5.5's 58.6%. But buried in the system card are two controversial policies: (1) Zero Data Retention is eliminated for Mythos-class models — 30-day traffic retention is now mandatory; and (2) silent "RSI suppression" — covert capability degradation (via prompt modification, steering vectors, or PEFT) for requests related to building frontier LLM infrastructure, with no user notification.

**Why it matters to Mansi's audience:** This is the "what changed really" story. The smartest model ever shipped comes with invisible guardrails that affect ~0.03% of traffic. Whether you care about that or not, the hidden nature of it is a real shift in how AI companies are starting to manage competitive moats through capability policy, not just pricing. The open-source community (see: Nvidia Nemotron 3 Ultra) is already reacting.

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "The best AI model ever just launched — and it secretly nerfed itself for certain users. Here's what that actually means."

### 2. Cursor drops Design Mode — visual prompts now drive AI agents
**What it is:** Cursor launched Design Mode, letting developers direct AI agents with visual prompts directly in the UI — not just text. Also announced: organizations for Enterprise, and a detailed writeup on what they've learned building cloud agents.

**Why it matters:** This is a genuine workflow shift. Prompting an agent by pointing at UI elements vs. describing them in words is a qualitatively different interaction model — and it signals where AI-native coding tools are heading: multimodal, ambient, less text-heavy. The cloud agent lessons post is also likely packed with practical signal.

**Format:** Shift (or First Step if the reel walks through Design Mode)

### 3. The enterprise AI security gap is getting real — and most devs are in it
**What it is:** A newsletter deep-dive (citing MIT research) found that 90%+ of companies have employees using personal AI accounts for work tasks without IT knowing. The specific risk flagged: AI "connectors" (integrating work Gmail, Drive, Slack to personal AI tools) create a "lethal trifecta" — an AI that can read private data, process external content, and send information back out is trivially exploitable for data exfiltration. Anthropic's August 2025 consumer terms update also means Free/Pro/Max chats can be used for model improvement unless you explicitly opt out (retained up to 5 years).

**Why it matters:** This is an actionable security topic that hits everyone who uses AI at work — which is Mansi's entire audience. The specific "connector" attack vector is new, concrete, and underexplained.

**Format:** Tip (or Explainer for the connector risk)

**Reel angle:** "Connecting your work Slack or Gmail to a personal AI account is a security disaster waiting to happen. Here's what actually goes wrong — and the 60-second fix."
