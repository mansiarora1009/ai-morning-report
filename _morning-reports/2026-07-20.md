# Morning Report — 2026-07-20

## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Kimi K3 and the death of the compute moat
**What it is:** Moonshot AI (China) launched Kimi K3 — a 2.8T parameter model that beats Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 High on benchmarks, prices like Anthropic Sonnet ($3/$15 per M tokens), and leads Arena.ai's Frontend Code arena above Claude Fable 5. Open weights promised by July 27. The community signal from AINews: the strategic argument has shifted from "compute moat" to "efficiency stack" — MoE routing, quantization, data curation, and scarcity-driven infra (Moonshot's "Mooncake" stack).

**Why it matters:** The thesis that frontier AI capability is gated mainly by raw FLOPs and Western capex is cracking. K3 shows Chinese labs are compressing the capability-per-FLOP curve rather than matching Western capex directly. For your audience: the model race now has a real challenger that's both open-weight and affordable — and it's not from OpenAI or Anthropic.

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "The compute moat just got smaller — China's Kimi K3 is frontier-class and going open source in a week"

### 2. Karpathy's 80/20 flip — what AI-native work actually looks like
**What it is:** Andrej Karpathy says he's now at a 20/80 ratio: 20% writing code himself, 80% delegating to agents. Six months ago it was flipped. He also controls his lights, HVAC, pool, and security through a single WhatsApp conversation with an agent he calls Dobby. Simon Willison noted that "agentic tool calling and the ability to operate tools reliably as conversations grow in length" is now the only thing that matters when comparing models.

**Why it matters:** This is what the frontier actually looks like — not a productivity bump, but a role inversion. For your audience, who use AI but haven't crossed this threshold: Karpathy's ratio is the map. The question isn't "how do I use AI more" but "what does it look like to let the agent drive?"

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "Karpathy went from 80% coding himself to 80% delegating to agents — here's what that actually looks like day-to-day"

### 3. Anthropic Engineering: Decoupling the brain from the hands
**What it is:** Anthropic Engineering published "Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands" — a technical breakdown of their architecture for running agents at scale. The key insight: separate the reasoning model (brain) from the tool-execution layer (hands), so each can scale and be optimized independently. Same week they published "How we contain Claude across products" — their safety/containment approach for multi-product agent deployments.

**Why it matters:** This is the architectural pattern behind real production agent systems, from someone who's shipping them. Your audience is building with AI and hitting walls when things go agentic — this is the map for why. The brain/hands split is an explainer-ready concept that makes "agents" finally make sense structurally.

**Format:** Explainer

**Reel angle:** "Why your AI agent keeps breaking — Anthropic just published the architecture that fixes it"
