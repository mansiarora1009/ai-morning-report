### 1. OpenAI is building a Palantir-style services company
**What it is:** OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company — a majority-owned unit built to embed AI into enterprise workflows. It comes with the acquisition of 150-person AI consulting firm Tomoro, 150 Forward Deployed Engineers, and $4B in initial investment from 19 partners. The model: technical staff who sit inside customer orgs and build, not just sell.

**Why it matters:** This is a full strategic pivot from "sell the model, let partners implement" to "we own the deployment layer." Multiple observers drew the Palantir comparison immediately. For Mansi's audience: this is the inflection point where AI moves from SaaS tool to professional services at scale. The people in tech who are already good at building with AI are about to become extremely valuable — because that's who OpenAI is now competing to hire and to sell to.

### 2. Thinking Machines shipped the first true interaction model — not a chatbot with voice bolted on
**What it is:** Thinking Machines (Mira Murati's company, emerging publicly for only the third time in a year) released TML-Interaction-Small — a 276B MoE model (12B active params) trained from scratch for real-time interaction. It processes continuous audio + video + text simultaneously in 200ms microturns. Key capabilities: time-aware speech initiation, interruption handling, code-switching, visual proactivity ("tell me when I start slouching", "count my reps"). Beats GPT-Realtime-2 and Gemini 3.1-Flash on multiple benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Every "realtime AI" demo so far has been a turn-based LLM with speech and vision tacked on. This model is architecturally different — the turn boundary doesn't exist. It's the first serious attempt to build what the GPT-4o "Her" demo promised but couldn't deliver. If this works in practice, it changes the interface assumption: AI stops being a thing you prompt and starts being a thing that's present.

**Reel angle:** "The turn-based chat interface finally has a replacement — here's what Thinking Machines actually shipped and why it's different from every voice AI demo you've seen"

### 3. OpenAI deprecated their finetuning API — and that's the right call
**What it is:** OpenAI is deprecating its finetuning APIs, following Sora as the second major "side quest" casualty of 2026. The AINews take: for the median 80% of AI engineers, finetuning was already being replaced by longer prompts + better base models. The exception: top companies like Cursor and Cognition have actually *increased* open-model RLFT. The divide is sharpening.

**Why it matters:** This is the clearest signal yet that the "customize the model" era is ending for most practitioners. The new primitive is context engineering — how you structure what you give the model, not how you retrain it. For Mansi's audience, who are still sorting through when to fine-tune vs. prompt: the answer just got simpler. Prompt better, pick the right model, build the right harness.

**Format:** Explainer

**Reel angle:** "OpenAI killed their finetuning API — here's what that tells you about where the real leverage is now"
