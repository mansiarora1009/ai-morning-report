## Top 3 Things Worth Paying Attention To

### 1. Claude Sonnet 5.5 shipped — 30% faster, 30% cheaper, and now the free tier model

**What it is:** Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5.5, the second model in the Claude 5.5 family (one week after Opus 5.5, the day before OpenAI DevDay). It runs 30%+ faster and costs 30% less than Sonnet 5, with early evals placing it near Opus 5.5 on several leaderboards. Available day-one on the Claude Platform, Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot VS Code, Cline, and Devin Desktop. Critically: it now powers the free tier on claude.ai — bumping free users from Sonnet 5.1. Haiku 5.5 coming in weeks.

There's a less-covered technical change worth noting: Anthropic extended "preserved thinking" as an anti-distillation measure. Reasoning traces now stay in the org that generated them — if a session moves to another account, Claude rereads and regenerates thinking rather than porting it over. This closes a loophole where orgs could switch accounts to extract reasoning traces for model distillation.

**Why it matters:** Two distinct angles. For practitioners: the model selection question is now simpler — Sonnet 5.5 for most everyday coding/iteration tasks, Opus 5.5 when you need maximum reasoning. Anthropic published a build guide covering the tradeoffs explicitly. For the audience: "the free tier is now Claude 5.5" is the kind of quiet infrastructure move that shifts what non-paying users can actually do.

### 2. Claude Mods is a real thing — and it might be the first glimpse of mutable software

**What it is:** The Latent Space podcast episode "Claude Code's Next Era" (with Anthropic's Thariq Shihipar) covers Claude Mods in depth: a system for customizing Claude Code's execution loop, UI, subagent routing, and behavior. You can write a mod that changes how agents are spawned, how tool use is routed, even fork agents mid-task. Thariq's framing: Claude.md may eventually disappear — not because prompts go away but because the harness itself becomes dynamic. The concept he calls "mutable software": applications that rewrite their own behavior based on context, rather than being statically deployed.

Also covered: "cloud brain, local hands" — Claude Code splitting into a cloud reasoning layer and local execution hands; Claude Projects and Tag for multiplayer agent workflows; and why spending more time on the initial prompt dramatically reduces wasted agent work.

**Why it matters:** Most of the audience is still at "I use Claude in a chat window." Claude Mods is three abstraction levels above that. The concrete unlock: you can build a mod that routes simple bugs to Sonnet 5.5 and complex architecture decisions to Opus 5.5 automatically. Or a mod that spins up a reviewer agent on every PR. The philosophical unlock: software that customizes itself is a paradigm shift, not just a feature. This is the "what changes really" story under the model release headlines.

**Format:** Explainer / Shift

**Reel angle:** "Everyone's talking about which Claude model to use — but Anthropic just shipped something that makes that question almost irrelevant"

### 3. Simon Willison named the thing software engineers are feeling: "Deep Blue"

**What it is:** Willison gave the closing keynote at WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America (San Jose), doing a "2026 in LLMs so far" retrospective. He traced coding agents crossing a reliability threshold in November 2025 (Claude Code + Codex improving from "often makes mistakes" to "reliable enough to use daily"), then walked through how that changed everything. His New Year's resolution: "be more ambitious" — the only way to find the limits of this technology is to keep pushing until it doesn't work.

The term coined on his podcast earlier in the year: **Deep Blue** — "that feeling of AI-induced ennui where software engineers get listless because the AI can do anything." He reports it's been a major theme at conferences throughout the year. Also from the keynote: 40 of the 277 sessions at the conference touched on sandboxing or agent security, a "Challenger disaster" from coding agents hasn't happened yet but the noise is real, and context of the new price war (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Luna) is part of this story.

**Why it matters:** "Deep Blue" is a name for something Mansi's audience knows viscerally but hasn't had language for. The engineering identity question — "what does my job mean when agents can do the work?" — is more relevant this week than any model spec. This is the highest-leverage explainer in the pipeline right now: it's relatable, it's real, and it leads directly into a productive reframe (the answer isn't "nothing matters," it's "be more ambitious").

**Format:** Shift

**Reel angle:** "Software engineers have a name for what they're feeling this year — it's called Deep Blue, and the inventor of the term just explained what it means for your career"
